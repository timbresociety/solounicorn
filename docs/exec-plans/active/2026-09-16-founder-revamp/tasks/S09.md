## Outcome and small checkpoints

1. Define ticket lifecycle: offered → bought (price paid once) → partially inspected → selectively claimed or abandoned → closed. Sample random patch outcomes once at issuance under a stable ticket ID and persist them.
2. Partial scratching exposes a reliable assessment cue without applying the outcome. Manual play can finish positive patches on a worthwhile card or abandon a bad card, retaining the ticket-price loss. No negative patch applies merely because it was inspected; intentional full resolution applies its outcome.
3. Automated resolution fully scratches all remaining patches and accepts their signed aggregate, including negatives. Persist already-claimed patches so manual/auto handoff cannot repay them. Model operational incidents/repairs and cash outcomes through typed finite effects.
4. Tune random rank-0 tickets and wider higher-Luck distributions. Measure manual selection and whole-ticket EV after price, then company viability after agent upkeep. Implement 16 Ops ranks with finite supply/time/capacity so positive EV supports scaling without zero-time arbitrage.

## Acceptance

- Purchase, inspect, claim, abandon, auto-resolve, reload and repeated inputs preserve price/outcome exactly once.
- Inspection never rerolls ticket values. Manual selection and automatic all-patch resolution are visibly different strategies.
- Negative mandatory outcomes use the resolved cash-failure policy; no silent unsigned/clamped loss.
- Seeded distributions show increasing variance and a viable positive-net automation path with tail risk. Free-inspection optimal strategy, finite supply, mixed handoff and correlated risks are evaluated.
- Repair effects target actual strain/rot/incidents; no disconnected coin generator or reinstated repair-only toy.
