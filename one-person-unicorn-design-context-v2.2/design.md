# 0. Reference hierarchy and interpretation

The references below are **quality bars and design principles, not templates to imitate**.  
Do not copy another studio's logo, layout, proprietary asset, interface, campaign, illustration, or recognizable visual signature. The goal is an original ONE PERSON UNICORN system with comparable rigor.

## 0.1 Binding hierarchy

When an agent makes a design decision, use this order:

1. **Canonical product truth** — mechanics, economics, one-pointer input, responsive/PWA constraints, simulation state.
2. **ONE PERSON UNICORN brand constants** — monograms, custom contextual 3D icon language, dark operational machine, Pastel Ethereal brand-art world, and Ethereal as the universal apex material only for rarity-bearing systems.
3. **Interaction clarity** — the player must understand input, consequence, pressure, and opportunity.
4. **Identity coherence** — typography, material, iconography, motion, spacing, image language.
5. **Reference principles** — external references guide quality, never literal output.
6. **Novelty** — only after the first five are satisfied.

## 0.2 What each external reference means

Interpret this as:
- decisive graphic hierarchy;
- instantly recognizable symbology;
- typography with authorship rather than default component-library styling;
- a brand system that can stretch from tiny digital surfaces to large-format campaigns;
- variation inside a stable institutional identity;
- strong composition, cropping, scale, rhythm, and negative space.

Do **not** imitate The Public Theater, Tiffany, Citi, or any specific Scher/Pentagram execution.

Interpret this as:

- the type system must remain simple enough that coding and generative agents can apply it correctly.

- every major flow should have **one defining emotional moment**, not constant spectacle;
- motion creates understanding, memory, and perceived quality;

# 0A. The central design resolution: MACHINE + SKY

ONE PERSON UNICORN has two deliberately different visual planes.

## A. THE MACHINE — product/gameplay
The founder operates an expensive, unstable machine.

Visual language:
- near-black graphite;
- matte, satin, brushed and polished metals;
- calm precision instrumentation;
- physical 3D work objects;
- contextual premium 3D objects without a forced universal texture;
- strong state feedback;
- compressed information when the player is under pressure.

Emotional function:
**control, pressure, competence, consequence.**

## B. THE SKY — brand/editorial/milestone
The company fantasy becomes larger than the operating surface.

Visual language:
- pastel ethereal worlds;
- blue, cyan, lavender, blush, pearlescent white;
- fog, atmospheric depth, reflective surfaces;
- monumental spatial compositions;
- sparse architecture / impossible environments;
- liquid-metal monograms or hero objects.

Emotional function:
**aspiration, transcendence, scale, myth.**

## C. BRIDGE MOMENTS
The two planes may merge only at emotionally important transitions:
- start-of-run identity reveal;
- major financing or company-stage transition;
- pinnacle skill-tree unlock;
- legendary luck outcome;
- Board Meeting;
- crossing $1B valuation;
- continue-company / post-unicorn state;
- major campaign/seasonal brand moments.

A bridge moment should feel like the dark machine briefly opens onto the ethereal world.

This duality prevents two common failures:
1. making the whole product a dark generic sci-fi dashboard;
2. making the whole game a soft pastel moodboard with weak gameplay readability.

## 1. Brand thesis

ONE PERSON UNICORN should feel like **a tiny founder operating an impossibly expensive machine**.

The visual world combines:
- industrial control systems;
- dark startup-terminal environments;
- toy-like tactile controls;
- high-value financial instrumentation;
- absurdly polished automation machinery;
- custom hyperrealistic 3D object imagery;
- contextual luxury, nostalgia, and contemporary cultural resonance;
- rare semantic material transformations only where progression or rarity needs to be communicated.

The brand should be recognizable even with the logo removed.

### Core recall device

The unique visual recall device is:

> **A dark precision machine, proprietary monograms, unusually thoughtful hyperrealistic 3D object imagery, and rare views into a pastel ethereal world.**

The signature memory structure is the **Dual Monogram System**.

The signature product-asset behavior is the **Contextual 3D Icon System**: navigation, menus, buttons, headers, labels, and other text-adjacent UI use custom hyperrealistic outlined 3D image assets on transparent backgrounds. These icons are not assigned a universal material. Their form and finish come from the meaning of the component.

The signature rarity endpoint is **Ethereal**: iridescent liquid metal. Ethereal belongs to rarity-bearing asset systems, not generic UI decoration.

Every important 3D object and icon should look art-directed for its exact context. Every brand surface should remain recognizable through some combination of **monogram, typography, contextual object language, composition, motion, or the MACHINE/SKY contrast**, even when the wordmark is absent.

The product should **not** look like:
- generic glassmorphism;
- cyberpunk neon;
- rainbow SaaS gradients;
- crypto/Web3 branding;
- gamer RGB;
- Apple Vision Pro imitation;
- flat corporate dashboard software;
- emoji-driven UI;
- stock icon-library software;
- a literal unicorn-themed product.

The "unicorn" is represented through **ambition, rarity, value, scale, taste, and progression**, not unicorn illustrations or a forced material treatment.

# 2. Design principles

## 2.1 Expensive object, simple interaction

Interactions should be immediately understandable even when the object looks visually premium.

A player should know what to:
- tap;
- swipe;
- merge;
- drag;
- scratch;
- aim;

without reading instructions.

Surface complexity is allowed. Interaction complexity is not.

## 2.2 The simulation is the hero

UI chrome should frame the game, not dominate it.

The highest visual emphasis belongs to:
1. the active work object;
2. immediate ARR/cash/valuation consequence;
3. crisis or opportunity;
4. current quarter state;
5. automation activity.

## 2.3 Material follows semantic role

Do **not** use material richness as a universal shortcut for UI importance.

There are two separate systems:

### Utility / interface objects
Navigation icons, menu icons, button icons, header icons, work-function icons and other text-adjacent 3D image assets use whatever physically believable materials best express their semantic object. They do not automatically become metal, glass, gold, or iridescent because they are important.

### Rarity-bearing objects
Relics, achievements, upgrades, difficulties, collectible tiers and other explicitly ranked asset systems may use material transformation to communicate progression. Their lower states are chosen semantically. Their final state is always **Ethereal**.

Never transfer the rarity material ladder onto ordinary UI.
## 2.4 Funny content, serious instrumentation

The world can be absurd.

The numbers must remain legible.

Financial and simulation data should use clean typography, stable positions, flat semantic colors, and restrained animation.

## 2.5 Tactility over decoration

Visual effects should imply:
- weight;
- friction;
- spring;
- magnetic attraction;
- machining;
- compression;
- impact;
- fluidity.

Avoid animation that exists only to make the interface "feel alive."

# 4. Rarity material system

Material progression is a **semantic progression system**, not a universal skin stack.

Use it only when an asset family communicates explicit rarity, achievement, upgrade level, difficulty, relic quality, collectible status, or a comparable ranked hierarchy.

## 4.1 Universal apex rule

**Ethereal is always the highest material state.**

Ethereal means **iridescent liquid metal**. It should read as the most evolved, rare, materially impossible and premium state in the progression.

Everything below Ethereal is flexible. Lower states may be added, removed, merged, reordered or renamed to fit the semantic journey of that asset family.

The design agent must ask internally:
1. What is this asset becoming as rarity increases?
2. What material transformation makes that progression intuitively legible?
3. Does each stage feel more resolved/evolved than the previous one?
4. Does Ethereal feel like the natural apex rather than a random rainbow skin?

## 4.2 Canonical example, not a fixed ladder

For an enlightenment progression, a valid four-state semantic ladder is:

```text
charcoal pitch-black stone
→ shiny gold
→ translucent liquid glass
→ Ethereal iridescent liquid metal
```

Meaning:
- charcoal stone = raw / dormant / unformed;
- gold = refined / disciplined / valuable;
- liquid glass = clarified / lucid / purified;
- Ethereal = transcendent / impossible / apex.

This exact ladder must **not** be mechanically reused for unrelated themes.

Other valid examples:

```text
Machinery:
cast iron → brushed steel → polished chrome → Ethereal

Organic:
raw bone/stone → amber/pearl → crystalline tissue → Ethereal

Cosmic:
dark mineral → stellar metal → astral crystal → Ethereal
```

Choose the progression that makes sense for the noun, fantasy and progression being represented.

# 5. Ethereal material specification

## 5.1 Definition

**Ethereal = iridescent liquid metal.**

Target surface behavior:
- liquid-metal macro-form or surface flow;
- strong reflective depth;
- physically convincing metallic response;
- controlled cyan / blue / violet / magenta / blush spectral interference;
- occasional warmer reflections when the scene supports them;
- high-quality specular highlights;
- materially impossible, but never cheap holographic plastic.

Avoid:
- rainbow gradient fill;
- glitter;
- holographic foil;
- soap-bubble translucency;
- neon gamer RGB;
- flat pearlescent plastic;
- texture noise that destroys the silhouette.

## 5.2 Spectral palette

These colors describe possible interference reflections. They are not mandatory flat fills.

| Token | Hex | Character |
|---|---:|---|
| `spectral-cyan` | `#62F0FF` | cold highlight |
| `spectral-blue` | `#6D8CFF` | electric transition |
| `spectral-violet` | `#A56CFF` | premium depth |
| `spectral-magenta` | `#FF5FD2` | hot reflection |
| `spectral-rose` | `#FF768D` | warm edge |
| `spectral-amber` | `#FFC45B` | occasional warm flare |

The material must read as **iridescent liquid metal**, not as a painted rainbow.

## 5.3 Rendering behavior

Preferred Ethereal rendering:
- large soft white key;
- controlled cool and warm environment reflections;
- high-contrast reflective sweep;
- enough dark reflection to preserve form;
- restrained bloom;
- subtle view-angle color change;
- clean silhouette before surface spectacle.

The founder-supplied bust references define the required surface logic. Use those references for **texture and material behavior only**, not anatomy, subject matter, composition, or object category.

# 6. Rarity progression rules

## 6.1 Tier count is contextual

A rarity system may have three, four, five, seven or another justified number of states. Do not invent extra materials merely to fill slots.

Examples:

```text
3 tiers: base → evolved → Ethereal
4 tiers: raw → refined → clarified → Ethereal
5+ tiers: add semantically meaningful intermediate transformations → Ethereal
```

## 6.2 Rarity progression test

A valid material ladder must satisfy all of the following:
- each state is visually distinguishable;
- each state is semantically explainable;
- the sequence feels directional;
- the same material is not reused merely because it looked premium elsewhere;
- Ethereal is always top;
- ordinary navigation/menu/button/header icons are excluded from this system unless the icon itself is representing a rarity-bearing item.

## 6.3 No forced texture inheritance

Rarity material choices do not create a global product texture hierarchy. A standard 3D navigation icon should remain whatever object/material best communicates its meaning, even if an achievement elsewhere uses stone → gold → glass → Ethereal.

# 8. No fake material shortcuts

Do not imitate Ethereal with a generic CSS rainbow gradient, holographic overlay, or glassmorphism treatment.

When an actual rarity-bearing asset requires Ethereal:
- render or generate the material as part of the object;
- preserve realistic lighting and reflection logic;
- preserve silhouette readability;
- keep the background transparent when the asset is used as an isolated image;
- do not convert ordinary UI into iridescent surfaces merely for consistency.

For ordinary UI, prefer clean product surfaces and contextual 3D imagery over simulated material effects.

# 11. Typography

## 11.1 Personality

Typography should feel:
- modern;
- condensed where useful;
- operational;
- financial;
- slightly editorial;
- never sci-fi.

## 11.2 Recommended font stack

### UI / numbers

**Inter** or another high-quality grotesk with tabular numerals.

```css
font-family:
  Inter,
  ui-sans-serif,
  -apple-system,
  BlinkMacSystemFont,
  "Segoe UI",
  sans-serif;
font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums;
```

### Display

Prefer a bold grotesk / neo-grotesk, not a techno typeface.

Brand voice comes from:
- scale;
- spacing;
- copy;
- material;

not novelty fonts.

## 11.3 Type scale

| Role | Mobile | Desktop | Weight |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Hero valuation | 40–48 | 56–72 | 700 |
| Primary metric | 24–30 | 28–36 | 650–700 |
| Room title | 20–24 | 24–30 | 650 |
| Body | 15–16 | 15–17 | 450–500 |
| Utility | 12–13 | 12–14 | 500 |
| Micro label | 10–11 | 10–12 | 600 |

Uppercase is allowed only for very short instrumentation labels.

## 11.4 Typographic authorship

The brand must not rely on a novelty font. Authorship comes from:
- deliberate scale jumps;
- strong width and weight contrast;
- asymmetric but controlled composition;
- confident cropping on campaign surfaces;
- exact line breaks;
- large financial numerals;
- disciplined negative space;
- recurring alignment rules.

A design agent must never “center everything and add a gradient.”

## 11.5 Font governance

The canonical system defines roles before filenames.

### Role A — Product Text
Requirements:
- excellent screen legibility;
- broad weight range;
- clear punctuation;
- strong lowercase;
- stable UI metrics;
- full tabular numerals.

Implementation fallback: **Inter**.

### Role B — Product Display / Campaign Grotesk
Requirements:
- strong display weight;
- width range or condensed companion preferred;
- works at very large scale;
- does not look technological or gamer-coded;
- pairs naturally with Product Text.

Implementation fallback: **Inter Tight**.

### Role C — Numeric Instrument
May be the same family as Product Text if tabular numerals are excellent.

Requirements:
- tabular numerals;
- distinguishable `0/O`, `1/l/I`;
- stable `$`, `%`, decimal and suffix behavior;
- excellent readability in dense HUD states.

### Optional Role D — Editorial Accent
Only for long-form brand/editorial moments, never gameplay-critical data.

If a future licensed/custom family is selected, replace the fallbacks globally. Do not mix ad-hoc fonts per screen.

## 11.6 Responsive typography rule

Type scale changes by **composition**, not proportional shrinking.

On compact screens:
- preserve primary metric legibility;
- collapse labels before shrinking numerals;
- reduce simultaneous secondary data;
- keep headings short;
- keep tap labels legible.

On wide screens:
- use additional space for hierarchy and relationships, not giant empty gutters between related controls.

## 11.7 Typographic QA

Reject a screen if:
- the same hierarchy could be produced by any default dashboard kit;
- more than three display emphases compete at once;
- important numerals move position unpredictably;
- microcopy is too small to preserve a “luxury” look;
- colored text replaces hierarchy that should come from size/weight/space.

# 13. Contextual 3D icon system

## 13.1 Core rule

Across navigation, menus, buttons, headers, labels and other text-adjacent UI, icons are **custom hyperrealistic outlined 3D objects rendered as images on transparent backgrounds**.

They exist to give visual appeal, semantic recall and authored personality to the text beside them.

**Strict prohibitions:**
- no flat icon system;
- no emoji;
- no stock icon library as the visible product language;
- no generic 3D symbol merely because it is technically correct;
- no unthoughtful literal object when a stronger cultural/nostalgic semantic exists;
- no forced Ethereal/gold/glass/stone texture on ordinary icons.

## 13.2 Required visual output

Every icon should be:
- hyperrealistic 3D;
- isolated on a transparent background;
- outlined through strong silhouette, edge separation and controlled rim/contour lighting rather than a cartoon stroke;
- internally lit/shaded so its dimensionality survives compositing;
- generated with its shadows, ambient occlusion, reflections and contact/self-shadow logic already considered;
- visually coherent beside product typography;
- readable quickly at its intended UI size;
- premium without unnecessary ornament.

Do not bake a card, colored square, gradient tile, scene, or opaque backdrop into the icon image unless the object itself semantically requires that geometry.

## 13.3 Semantic-generation process

For every icon, the design agent must internally resolve this sequence:

```text
PRODUCT CONTEXT
→ EXACT MEANING OF THE WORD/PHRASE HERE
→ MOST CULTURALLY CURRENT / POP-CULTURE-RESONANT OBJECT
→ MOST NOSTALGIC / ARCHETYPAL RESONANT OBJECT
→ ORIGINAL SEMANTIC FUSION
→ ELITE / COLLECTIBLE ART DIRECTION
→ SMALL-SIZE SILHOUETTE TEST
```

The two source objects should not simply sit next to each other. Prefer a coherent fusion where one becomes the structure, mechanism, silhouette or functional detail of the other.

## 13.4 Culture-pulse rule

The semantic choice is time-sensitive. Before generating an important new icon family, understand what currently resonates culturally around that concept, then balance it against a nostalgic object with durable recognition.

Do not chase memes blindly. The goal is **recognition + surprise + taste**.

Examples:
- Camera = premium modern mirrorless camera × tactile analog rangefinder/SLR heritage.
- Search = modern optical scanner × detective loupe/monocle.
- Services = contemporary automation assistant × classic concierge bell / serving ritual.
- Home = architecturally current home × heirloom key / domestic symbol.
- Messages = modern chat object × folded letter / correspondence artifact.

## 13.5 Elitism / premium-object rule

The icon should feel like the most desirable version of its semantic object:
- excellent materials where context warrants them;
- precision construction;
- tasteful proportions;
- sophisticated detailing;
- product-photography-grade lighting;
- collectible-object presence.

"Elite" does not mean putting gold on everything. Luxury should come from art direction, craft, specificity, restraint and quality of the fusion.

## 13.6 Texture rule

Use the material the object would naturally need to communicate its semantic fusion.

Examples:
- camera may use leatherette, anodized metal, glass optics, knurled controls;
- notebook may use paper, linen, leather, metal binding;
- search loupe may use optical glass and precision metal;
- home icon may use stone, glass, wood and a metal key.

Do **not** pull from the rarity material ladder unless the icon is specifically depicting a rarity-bearing relic, achievement, upgrade, difficulty or comparable ranked asset.

## 13.7 Construction and family consistency

Each icon should have:
- one dominant silhouette;
- one primary semantic fusion;
- at most 2–3 important secondary forms;
- deliberate camera angle;
- consistent perceived scale within a family;
- consistent studio-light quality;
- enough negative space around the object for compositing;
- no illegible micro-detail.

Family consistency comes from **render quality, camera discipline, silhouette clarity and art direction**, not from forcing all icons into the same material.

## 13.8 UI integration

The icon is an image asset. The UI component remains responsible for:
- hit target;
- selected state;
- focus state;
- badge;
- label;
- disabled treatment;
- motion/transition.

Do not rasterize UI text into the generated icon.

## 13.9 Approval test

Reject an icon if any answer is "no":
1. Does it communicate the correct in-product meaning?
2. Is there a thoughtful current-culture semantic?
3. Is there a thoughtful nostalgic/archetypal semantic?
4. Are those ideas fused rather than collaged?
5. Does the object feel premium and authored?
6. Does it work on transparent background?
7. Does the silhouette survive at the intended size?
8. Are the materials contextually justified?
9. Would it still feel considered if all special effects were removed?
10. Is it clearly superior to using a stock icon or emoji?

# 14. 3D asset art direction

All meaningful 3D assets should appear to belong to one manufactured universe.

## 14.1 Global asset rules

Every asset must define:
1. silhouette;
2. weight;
3. interaction affordance;
4. contextually appropriate material logic;
5. room accent;
6. motion state;
7. pressed / activated state;
8. damaged / overloaded state where relevant.

## 14.2 Material logic by asset class

### Utility / navigation 3D icons
Use materials that make the semantic object believable and premium. No universal texture.

### Interactive gameplay objects
Use materials that communicate affordance, weight, contact, wear, machinery and state. Material choices should support interaction comprehension.

### Rarity-bearing assets
Use a semantic material progression whose final state is Ethereal. See §§4–6.

### Emissive / danger states
Use semantic state color for warning, processing, success or failure. State communication must remain readable regardless of the base object material.

Avoid adding multiple premium materials simply to make an object look expensive.
## 14.3 Visual wear

The company is a machine under pressure.

As complexity increases, assets may gain:
- fingerprints;
- fine scratches;
- thermal discoloration;
- warning decals;
- vibration;
- cable clutter;
- temporary tape;
- stress lights.

Do not make assets dirty by default.

Pristine → strained should be visually meaningful progression.

# 15. Luck / mayhem art direction

Luck should feel like **the machine has become unstable and unusually reflective**.

When a luck mechanic activates:
- spectral range widens;
- reflections move faster;
- object deformation increases slightly;
- sound becomes brighter;
- small probability markers orbit or flicker;
- room lighting becomes less stable.

It should feel tempting and dangerous.

Do not use:
- casino chips;
- slot-machine fruit;
- literal roulette wheels unless mechanically necessary;
- rainbow confetti as the default reward effect.

# 16. Cards and roguelike upgrades

Roguelike cards must remain separate from skill-tree presentation.

## 16.1 Card body

```text
Base: ink-900
Frame: metallic, material tier based on rarity
Art: one strong contextual object/scene
Title: high contrast
Effect: plain-English mechanical impact
Flavor: one short funny line
```

## 16.2 Rarity

Rarity may be communicated through a semantically coherent material transformation, not arbitrary rainbow borders. Ethereal is always the highest material state when material progression is used.

| Rarity | Material / frame rule |
|---|---|
| Lower tiers | Contextual base/refinement states chosen for that card family |
| Intermediate tiers | Visibly more evolved states with semantic continuity |
| Highest tier | **Ethereal** + unique motion when appropriate |

Highest-rarity cards may use Ethereal material behavior and unique motion, but only when the card rarity system actually uses a material progression.

# 17. Skill trees

Skill trees should feel like **an industrial upgrade board**, not a fantasy RPG constellation.

Visual model:
- machined sockets;
- pipes / buses / channels;
- solid nodes;
- visible dependency paths;
- node shells may visually evolve through a semantic progression; if the tree uses explicit rarity materials, its apex state is Ethereal;
- active branch glows in room color.

The player should understand:
- what is unlocked;
- what is next;
- what branch they are committing to;
- exact mechanical effect;

without hover dependence.

Mobile must support tap-first inspection.

## 17.1 Skill-tree grammar

Each work-function tree is visually organized around the canonical progression dimensions:

- **Craft** — founder/manual execution becomes more powerful, precise, and expressive.
- **Scale** — throughput, parallelism, and economic leverage increase.
- **Autonomy** — agents absorb more decision/execution burden while introducing reliability and complexity tradeoffs.
- **Luck / Variance** — higher upside and higher mayhem, calibrated so the expected-value proposition remains understandable.

The exact underlying mechanics come from product canon. Design must never invent economic effects.

Visual distinction:
- Craft = dense tactile tooling / hand-controlled socket language.
- Scale = branching buses / multiplied channels / parallel hardware.
- Autonomy = agent cores / routing nodes / closed loops.
- Luck = optically unstable variance material / warped reflection / variance marker.

The branches must be distinguishable without relying only on color.

## 17.2 Roguelike cards versus skill trees

Never make upgrade cards look like skill-tree nodes.

Skill tree:
- persistent;
- structural;
- planned;
- navigable;
- dependency-driven.

Roguelike card:
- run-specific;
- surprising;
- contextual;
- immediately evaluable;
- chosen against two alternatives.

Every card must answer in under roughly two seconds:
1. **What changes?**
2. **How big is the change?**
3. **What is the catch, if any?**

Flavor copy comes after mechanical comprehension.

# 17A. Game UX doctrine

ONE PERSON UNICORN is not a dashboard with minigames attached.  
The player must **feel the causal machine**.

## 17A.1 Canonical interaction verbs

Each primary function has a simple physical verb:

| Function | Core verb | Interaction fantasy |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | **SWIPE** | judge attention opportunities |
| Product | **MERGE** | combine/build activation output |
| Monetization | **TAP** | convert/prioritize value capture |
| Retention | **AIM / AUTO-FIRE** | defend value against churn threats |
| Expansion | **DRAG / PACK** | fit cross-sell opportunities into limited capacity |
| Operations | **SCRATCH / REVEAL** | expose hidden operational costs/risk |

All must work with one pointer and a thumb.

No design agent may replace these with a generic click-card interaction merely because it is easier to implement.

## 17A.2 Interaction design schema

Whenever designing a room mechanic, interaction, card, modal, or new generative UX, define:

```text
PLAYER FANTASY
↓
INPUT VERB
↓
IMMEDIATE PHYSICAL RESPONSE
↓
SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE
↓
VISIBLE ECONOMIC / PRESSURE EFFECT
↓
NEW PROBLEM OR OPPORTUNITY
↓
NEXT PLAYER DECISION
```

If one link is missing, the interaction is incomplete.

## 17A.3 Causality rule

After a meaningful action, the player should be able to answer:
- What did I do?
- What changed?
- Why did it change?
- What did it create elsewhere?
- What should I care about next?

Use animation, spatial flow, ledger deltas, queue movement and short labels to preserve this causal model.

Do not hide important state changes behind flavor text.

## 17A.4 Automation must be spatially believable

Agents automate **the same work** the founder once handled.

When automation is introduced:
- work objects visibly enter the agent's process;
- processing state is visible;
- completion/failure routes are visible;
- throughput becomes physically legible;
- reliability problems leave readable evidence;
- the user can compare founder effort versus machine effort.

The fantasy is not “+12% automation.”  
The fantasy is **watching the company learn to operate itself, then watching that automation create new forms of pressure.**

## 17A.5 Complexity and strain must be visible

Complexity is not a decorative meter.

As complexity/strain rises, the world may show:
- longer queues;
- more cross-room routing;
- warning states;
- cable/connection density;
- machine vibration;
- temporary workarounds;
- less pristine surfaces;
- delayed processing;
- more simultaneous alerts.

Visual degradation must remain bounded. Never make high-pressure states unreadable.

## 17A.6 Readability under chaos

The game may become chaotic. The interface may not.

At peak pressure:
1. critical crisis state;
2. immediate action target;
3. ARR / valuation / cash consequence;
4. quarter survival state;
5. background automation;

must remain distinguishable.

## 17A.7 Mastery

First-run play:
- input is discoverable from shape and motion;
- feedback is immediate;
- labels explain consequence.

Experienced play:
- gesture path is shorter;
- evaluation becomes faster;
- queues and patterns become anticipatory;
- animation never blocks input;
- repeated explanatory text recedes.

The system should become **faster to read as the player becomes better**, not more verbose.

# 17B. Progressive disclosure and micro-tutorials

Complexity is allowed. **Initial complexity exposure is not.**

The game should teach the player the way a good coach teaches a complex sport: one meaningful interaction, one consequence, then the next layer only when the player has a reason to care about it.

## 17B.1 Teach in the order the player needs knowledge

Default teaching sequence:

```text
DO ONE THING
↓
SEE THE PHYSICAL RESPONSE
↓
SEE THE ECONOMIC / SYSTEM CONSEQUENCE
↓
UNDERSTAND THE LOCAL OBJECTIVE
↓
REPEAT IN A SLIGHTLY HARDER CONTEXT
↓
ONLY THEN INTRODUCE THE NEXT SYSTEM
```

The chess analogy is canonical:
- first teach how a piece moves by letting the player move it;
- then teach what the player is trying to accomplish;
- teach advanced exceptions such as castling or en passant only when the situation exists and the information becomes useful.

Do not teach systems in database order. Teach them in **decision order**.

## 17B.2 Micro-tutorial contract

A new mechanic should normally be introduced through a micro-tutorial with three beats:

1. **Point** — make the interactive object or opportunity unmistakable.
2. **Do** — require or invite the smallest meaningful player action.
3. **Prove** — visibly show what changed and why it mattered.

Only after the player has acted may explanatory copy expand on nuance.

A tutorial is incomplete if the user successfully follows an arrow but cannot explain the consequence of the action.

## 17B.3 First-use contextual teaching

Advanced rules, exceptions, automation states, uncommon hazards, and special-case interactions should be taught **at first relevance**, not at boot.

Examples:
- explain an agent failure when the first meaningful agent failure occurs;
- explain a newly unlocked skill-tree branch when the player first has a real choice involving it;
- explain a special Operations trap when the player first encounters the condition that can trigger it;
- explain a new valuation interaction at the first quarter in which it can materially change the outcome.

Do not make the player memorize future exceptions before the base loop is understood.

## 17B.4 Tutorial decay

Repeated help should decay with demonstrated competence:

```text
first encounter: visual cue + one-line instruction + consequence feedback
second encounter: shorter cue
later encounter: contextual nudge only when hesitation/error suggests need
mastered: no interruption; help remains replayable/on-demand
```

The player must always be able to revisit essential rules, controls, and previously shown tutorials.

## 17B.5 Safe practice

When a mechanic is unfamiliar and failure would be expensive, the first interaction should be:
- low stakes;
- reversible where possible;
- short;
- mechanically authentic rather than a fake slideshow.

The product should prefer **playable teaching** over tutorial carousels.

## 17B.6 Measure comprehension, not tutorial completion

Tutorial analytics should distinguish:
- player saw instruction;
- player performed input;
- player produced intended outcome;
- player later reproduced the behavior without prompting.

A tutorial completion event does not prove understanding.

# 17C. Attention architecture

The game must be stimulating without becoming interruptive.

The core principle is:

> **Stable peripheral awareness. Protected central agency. Escalating but player-pulled attention.**

## 17C.1 The center is gameplay territory

During active gameplay, the central area is reserved for:
- the current work interaction;
- the current decision target;
- the causal response to the player's action;
- high-value game feel;
- rare milestone moments when the game is already safely paused or transitioning.

Do not place ordinary notifications, explanatory cards, reward popups, system announcements, sales prompts, or tutorial paragraphs over the active central interaction.

A center-screen modal during an active loop is a design failure unless the interaction itself requires central focus.

## 17C.2 Peripheral frame

Important persistent state should live in a stable frame around the gameplay canvas.

The exact responsive composition may change, but the conceptual zones remain stable:

- **Header:** company survival and score state — valuation, ARR, cash, quarter / run phase, critical global status.
- **Left edge:** room/function navigation, current system context, high-level progression navigation where space permits.
- **Right edge:** alerts, queues, agents, contextual consequences, unresolved events, or secondary operational state.
- **Footer:** current action affordances, hand/cards/loadout/controls, contextual verbs, or immediate input support where the mechanic requires them.
- **Center:** gameplay and the current object of attention.

On mobile these zones may collapse into rails, drawers, compact stacks, or anchored controls. The **information architecture remains stable even when the geometry recomposes.**

## 17C.3 Peripheral UI may collapse when it stops helping

Persistent UI is not dogma. Chrome may temporarily reduce or hide when:
- the same information is not decision-relevant for the current interaction;
- a milestone/board-meeting state intentionally suspends normal operation;
- small-screen space would otherwise compromise the core interaction;
- a focused inspection screen provides the same state more legibly elsewhere.

When hidden, it should return predictably without the player having to rediscover navigation.

## 17C.4 Central alert inbox

ONE PERSON UNICORN should prefer a **persistent alert inbox / alert object** over forced modal interruption.

The alert object may sit near the central safe zone or a stable peripheral anchor close enough to be noticed without covering the active work surface.

Alerts accumulate into that object. The player chooses when to open the detailed queue unless immediate interaction is mechanically required.

### Alert escalation ladder

Use escalation progressively:

**Level 0 — ambient**
- badge/count/state only;
- no sound;
- no movement beyond normal idle behavior.

**Level 1 — relevant**
- short local pulse or object reaction;
- subtle UI earcon;
- one semantic color/state cue.

**Level 2 — important**
- repeated but spaced motion;
- stronger edge lighting or object posture change;
- distinct alert sound;
- optional short text summary near the inbox, not over gameplay.

**Level 3 — urgent**
- visibly more vigorous movement or mechanical instability;
- persistent but non-masking audio motif at a controlled cadence;
- stronger contrast and/or haptic cue;
- relevant perimeter state may join the warning.

**Level 4 — immediate gameplay requirement**
- only if the game rules genuinely require an immediate central decision or the current state is already paused;
- may move into the center because the alert has become the gameplay.

Do not jump straight to Level 3 for routine events.

## 17C.5 Alarm-fatigue rule

When everything is urgent, nothing is urgent.

The alert system must:
- coalesce repeated events;
- increase intensity only when consequence increases;
- stop escalating once acknowledged;
- avoid replaying the same earcon on every duplicate event;
- use distinct motifs for distinct categories rather than louder versions of one generic alarm;
- let resolved events visibly settle.

## 17C.6 Interruption rules

Center interruption MAY occur for:
- a mechanic whose input is itself a center-screen event/QTE;
- an irreversible destructive action requiring confirmation;
- a quarter/board-meeting/milestone transition where active gameplay is already suspended;
- legal/account/security flows that genuinely require blocking completion.

Center interruption MUST NOT occur merely because:
- a reward arrived;
- a queue changed;
- a new upgrade became available;
- an agent finished something;
- a marketing/sales prompt wants attention;
- a tutorial designer wants the player to read text.

## 17C.7 Reorientation after intensity

After a high-stimulation moment, the game should quickly restore orientation:
- the active target becomes visually dominant again;
- peripheral motion settles;
- the causal delta remains visible long enough to understand;
- the next meaningful decision is apparent;
- control is already available or returns promptly.

Do not end a big reward with five new competing calls to action.

# 17D. Stimulation doctrine and event budget

ONE PERSON UNICORN is allowed to feel busy, juicy, funny, chaotic and high-energy. The interface is not allowed to become unreadable.

The product should target **orchestrated stimulation**, not low stimulation and not stimulus spam.

## 17D.1 Stimulation is multi-channel

The game can stimulate through:
- physical object motion;
- score/number deltas;
- particles;
- lighting;
- sound effects;
- music arrangement;
- haptics;
- short reactive copy;
- queue movement;
- character/object reactions;
- camera movement;
- rarity transformation;
- reward cadence.

These channels should work as an ensemble. They should not all peak at once for every event.

## 17D.2 Event priority tiers

Use this canonical hierarchy:

| Tier | Event type | Example response |
|---|---|---|
| **P0** | ambient operation | tiny motion, machine hum, passive numbers |
| **P1** | routine player action | immediate microphysics + concise SFX + local delta |
| **P2** | meaningful success/failure | stronger motion + clear number/state change + one accent channel |
| **P3** | critical operational event | alert-inbox escalation + distinct audio/haptic + focused perimeter change |
| **P4** | milestone / run-defining event | brief authored choreography, music shift, larger visual payoff, safe center use |

Routine events must not borrow P4 language.

## 17D.3 One lead channel

For any event above P1, choose a **lead sensory channel**.

Examples:
- merge success: motion leads, SFX supports, particles finish;
- financial warning: number/state contrast leads, audio supports;
- alert escalation: movement rhythm leads, audio cadence supports;
- major unlock: object transformation leads, music and haptic reinforce;
- $1B valuation: composition and music lead, everything else follows.

This prevents the entire UI from shouting at once.

## 17D.4 Contrast is part of stimulation

High stimulation only feels high when lower-intensity states exist.

Use:
- quieter operational stretches;
- smaller routine effects;
- moments where music thins;
- reduced particle density after peaks;
- visual stillness around an important number;
- brief silence before/after exceptional outcomes.

Do not maintain maximum energy continuously.

## 17D.5 Repetition compression

When many similar events occur rapidly:
- aggregate number deltas;
- combine particles into one readable burst;
- reduce repeated sound density;
- let the queue or counter communicate scale;
- promote only the exceptional event to a higher tier.

Do not fire identical full-feedback packages dozens of times per second.

## 17D.6 Player-controlled intensity

Provide settings for:
- reduced motion;
- screen-shake intensity;
- flash/effect intensity;
- particle density where technically useful;
- alert intensity;
- haptic strength/on-off;
- music, SFX, UI, alert and voice volume where applicable.

Reducing sensory intensity must not remove mechanical information.

# 17E. Copywriting, naming and cultural literacy

The product's language is part of the game design.

Mechanical systems may be mathematically generic. **Player-facing language must not feel generic.**

## 17E.1 Copy hierarchy

Player-facing copy should resolve in this order:

```text
CLARITY
↓
CONTEXT
↓
PERSONALITY
↓
CULTURAL REWARD
```

A joke or reference that hides the mechanic is worse than plain language.

A mechanically clear line with no personality is acceptable as an internal placeholder, not as final authored game copy.

## 17E.2 Two-layer writing

Preferred pattern:

**Memorable name / headline**  
Plain-language mechanical meaning.

Examples of the pattern, not canonical final strings:

- **Post While You Sleep**  
  Agent handles 20% more Marketing work this quarter.

- **Founder Math**  
  Higher upside, higher variance. Expected value stays slightly positive.

- **Everything Is Fine™**  
  Operations pressure rises faster, but crisis rewards improve.

- **Ship It Friday**  
  Faster Product throughput. Failures cost more to recover.

The wit earns attention; the subtitle protects comprehension.

## 17E.3 “Elite ball knowledge” rule

Deep cultural literacy is encouraged when it creates recognition, surprise, specificity or social shareability.

A reference is successful when:
- a knowledgeable player gets an extra layer of delight;
- an unfamiliar player still understands the mechanic;
- the phrase fits the object/system naturally;
- it is not dependent on a trademarked quote, copied meme asset, or protected character;
- it will not become meaningless the moment a trend fades.

Use culture as **bonus meaning**, never required meaning.

## 17E.4 Current culture versus durable language

Core navigation and foundational systems should skew durable.

Run-specific cards, events, encounters, achievements, flavor lines and generated content can be more contemporary.

The more permanent the UI location, the less it should depend on a fast-aging meme.

## 17E.5 Domain specificity

Prefer language drawn from:
- startup/operator behavior;
- product and growth culture;
- internet business history;
- finance and fundraising;
- engineering and operations;
- founder folklore;
- adjacent pop culture when the semantic fit is strong.

Do not use “funny” language that could have been pasted into any roguelite.

## 17E.6 Concision

Default copy targets:
- navigation labels: 1–3 words;
- action buttons: verb-led and immediately scannable;
- alerts: one sentence or less before expansion;
- card mechanical description: readable in roughly two seconds;
- tooltip: mechanical meaning first, flavor second;
- tutorial instruction: one action at a time.

Do not write a paragraph where a state change plus six words can teach the same thing.

## 17E.7 Localization-aware authorship

Cultural writing must be built so it can be localized rather than hard-coded as English cleverness.

Requirements:
- every string has a stable ID;
- localization context describes the mechanic, tone, speaker and reference intent;
- avoid embedding text inside generated image assets;
- UI must tolerate reasonable text expansion;
- references that do not travel should have a transcreation note or locale-specific alternative;
- never concatenate sentence fragments in code where grammar may change across languages.

When a joke cannot survive translation, preserve the **function of the joke**, not the literal wording.

## 17E.8 Copy QA

Reject copy when:
- the mechanic is ambiguous;
- the same joke format appears everywhere;
- slang feels bolted on;
- the reference is doing all the work;
- the phrase is culturally specific without a clear fallback;
- it reads like generic AI banter;
- it is trying too hard to sound online;
- it repeats information already obvious from the UI.

# 18. Motion language

## 18.1 Default physics

| Motion | Character |
|---|---|
| Hover | subtle magnetic lift |
| Tap | 80–120 ms compression |
| Release | spring return |
| Merge | magnetic snap + mass conservation |
| Reward | slow reflective sweep + quick impact |
| Error | short mechanical jam |
| Crisis | vibration / instability |
| Automation | repetitive efficient machine cadence |

## 18.2 Timing

Typical:
- micro response: 80–140 ms
- state change: 180–260 ms
- panel transition: 220–320 ms
- major reward: 500–900 ms
- cinematic / milestone: max ~1.5 s before control returns

Avoid slow decorative UI.

## 18.3 Ethereal motion — rarity assets only

When an explicitly rarity-bearing asset is in its Ethereal state, the iridescent liquid-metal response should normally react to:
- pointer movement;
- device tilt where appropriate;
- object rotation;
- light movement;
- state change.

Never propagate this treatment into ordinary UI. Avoid continuously looping rainbow motion even on Ethereal assets; the response should feel physically caused by light, camera, pointer, state, or motion.

## 18.4 One defining emotional moment

Every major flow may have **one** dominant emotional beat.

Examples:

### First agent hired
Normal operation briefly slows; the same work object the founder used to handle routes into the agent for the first time. The machine completes it autonomously. The emotional beat is **relief becoming visible**.

### Agent failure
Do not show a generic red toast. Let a work object route incorrectly, jam, duplicate, or spill into a visible failure path before the error state resolves. The emotional beat is **trust breaking**.

### Legendary roguelike upgrade
Card selection compresses, reflection sweeps once, the selected object gains physical mass/charge, and the rule change is demonstrated. The emotional beat is **the build becoming something new**.

### Board Meeting
Operational noise recedes; the financial instrument becomes dominant. The player sees the run translated into business consequence. The emotional beat is **judgment**.

### $1B valuation
This is a bridge from Machine → Sky:
- dark operational sound falls away;
- the monogram/material opens into the pastel ethereal world;
- valuation becomes monumental typography;
- the moment is celebratory but controlled;
- control returns promptly.

The emotional beat is **the impossible becoming real**.

## 18.5 Motion hierarchy

Three layers:

### Layer 1 — Microphysics
8–300 ms.
Press, release, hover, snap, drag resistance, focus, error acknowledgement.

### Layer 2 — Operational choreography
200–900 ms.
Work routing, agent processing, queue movement, room transitions, card selection, node activation.

### Layer 3 — Milestone cinema
500–1500 ms before useful control returns.
Major unlocks, quarter results, legendary outcomes, unicorn crossing.

Do not run Layer 3 behavior for routine events.

## 18.6 Motion must preserve agency

- Input feedback starts immediately.
- Visual completion may continue after the command is accepted.
- Never delay authoritative input because an ornamental animation has not ended.
- Any cinematic can be shortened/skipped once learned, except where a brief timing beat is mechanically required.
- Reduced motion changes presentation, never rules.

# 19. Audio, music and haptics

Sound is gameplay information, tactile reinforcement and emotional pacing. It is not decoration added at the end.

## 19.1 Sonic hierarchy

Use five functional layers:

1. **Ambient machine bed** — low-priority operational texture, room tone, automation hum.
2. **UI tactility** — clicks, snaps, drags, confirmations, navigation movement.
3. **Gameplay action** — merge, hit, conversion, scratch, pack, queue/process events.
4. **Alerts** — distinct motifs for meaningful risk, opportunity and crisis.
5. **Milestone / score music** — quarter outcomes, run-defining upgrades, unicorn crossing, major transitions.

The mix must preserve the hierarchy. Routine UI should not mask gameplay state. Ambient sound should not mask alerts. Alerts should not become a permanent siren bed.

## 19.2 Sonic identity

Preferred sonic vocabulary:
- dense metallic clicks;
- short hydraulic compression;
- magnetic snaps;
- brushed metal scrapes;
- tactile rubber/plastic contact where the object calls for it;
- soft synthesized financial ticks;
- low machinery hum under automation;
- brittle/glassy transients only for objects that are actually glass-like;
- rare high-frequency spectral shimmer for Ethereal rarity assets.

Avoid generic:
- casino reward jingles;
- retro arcade bleeps everywhere;
- fantasy magic sparkles;
- constant EDM risers;
- cinematic braams for routine events.

## 19.3 Audio communicates state

Important objects/events should have distinguishable sounds.

A player should increasingly be able to infer what happened from sound without looking, but **no essential information may be conveyed by audio alone**.

Pair critical audio with:
- state change;
- text/icon cue;
- motion;
- haptic when appropriate.

## 19.4 Alert audio escalation

Alert sound follows the same levels as §17C.4.

- Level 0: silent.
- Level 1: one short local earcon.
- Level 2: clearer motif, spaced repetition only if unresolved.
- Level 3: persistent controlled cadence with stronger timbral tension; still subordinate to direct gameplay SFX.
- Level 4: may become the lead channel because the alert is now the gameplay.

Acknowledging an alert should immediately reduce or stop its repeated audio.

Do not make urgency equal loudness alone.

## 19.5 Adaptive music

Music should react to game state through arrangement before it reacts through volume.

Recommended layer logic:
- **base pulse:** normal operation;
- **throughput layer:** stronger rhythm as automation/company throughput rises;
- **pressure layer:** harmonic/timbral tension as strain, burn or crisis risk rises;
- **opportunity layer:** brighter motif when a high-value opening appears;
- **milestone layer:** authored transition for board meetings, major unlocks and unicorn moments.

Layers may enter/leave gradually so the score feels tied to the state of the machine.

Do not play “boss music” merely because a meter is high.

## 19.6 Contrast and silence

Silence is a design tool.

Before or after a rare high-value moment, it may be more powerful to:
- thin the music;
- remove ambience;
- let one mechanical sound land cleanly;
- delay the full reward layer until the consequence is understood.

Do not fill every second with maximum musical density.

## 19.7 Repetition and fatigue

Frequently repeated sounds should support variation through:
- multiple samples where available;
- small pitch/timing variation when semantically appropriate;
- aggregation when many identical events happen together;
- rate limiting for repetitive alerts;
- dynamic mixing based on event importance.

The exact randomization values are implementation heuristics, not brand constants. The goal is to prevent a useful cue from becoming noise.

## 19.8 Mixing and ducking

Use sidechain/ducking only to reveal information, not as an effect by default.

Heuristic starting points for implementation testing:
- important alert may duck ambience/music by roughly 2–4 dB for a brief window;
- milestone transition may duck normal operation by roughly 4–8 dB before the new layer resolves;
- ordinary UI should generally not duck the mix.

These values are starting points, not research facts. Tune by playtest on laptop speakers, phone speakers and headphones.

## 19.9 Mobile audio

Critical cues must survive:
- mono playback;
- phone speakers with weak bass;
- moderate background noise;
- low device volume.

Do not encode important distinctions only in sub-bass or stereo position.

## 19.10 Audio accessibility

Provide separate controls where the game uses these channels:
- Master;
- Music;
- SFX / gameplay;
- UI;
- Alerts;
- Voice;
- Ambience.

Also provide:
- mute states that do not remove mechanical information;
- visual/caption equivalents for significant sound events;
- saved preferences;
- alert-intensity control where practical.

## 19.11 Haptics

Haptics should reinforce physical meaning:
- light tap: UI commit;
- short crisp pulse: successful snap/merge;
- heavier brief pulse: significant reward/impact;
- irregular restrained pulse: crisis/instability;
- no haptic for passive background updates.

Provide a haptic toggle and, where technically reasonable, intensity control.

Do not vibrate continuously because the screen is busy.

- richer secondary metrics;

Prefer DOM for:
- ARR;
- valuation;
- cash;
- quarter/time;
- critical crisis state;
- financing;
- upgrade descriptions;
- menus;
- accessibility labels;
- settings;
- tooltips;

Use Canvas/WebGL for:
- tactile room interaction;
- work-object choreography;
- physical feedback;
- effects;
- presentation.

Do not put all UI in Canvas because the minigames use Canvas.

## 20.8 Pointer contract

All canonical gameplay must work with one pointer:
- mouse;
- touch;
- pen;
- trackpad-derived pointer.

No canonical interaction requires:
- hover;
- right-click;
- keyboard shortcut;
- multi-touch;
- precision unavailable to a thumb.

DOM target guideline:
`44 × 44 CSS px` minimum.

Canvas hit areas must provide comparable practical touchability.

## 20.9 Rendering is not simulation

Visual resize, DPR, browser zoom, orientation, installed-window size, or reduced presentation quality must never:
- alter economic outcomes;
- reroll opportunity state;
- move authoritative work objects;
- alter timers;
- alter hit windows in logical game space;
- change ARR/cash/valuation;
- change replay outcome.

Presentation may degrade before simulation.

Target:
- authoritative simulation: deterministic, independent of viewport;
- presentation: target 60 FPS on normal supported hardware.

# 24. Canonical identity: Dual Monogram System

The founder-supplied two-monogram family supersedes all earlier speculative logo directions.

There are exactly two core marks.

## 24.1 Primary monogram — STRUCTURE

Form:
- compact cube / square-hex silhouette;
- built from four outward structural lobes and inward concave arcs;
- central star/diamond-shaped negative-space logic;
- strong vertical axis;
- reads as a contained machine / architecture / system.

Meaning:
**structure, company, compounding system, container, engineered value.**

Use as:
- primary app/brand emblem;
- favicon/app icon when legible;
- card back;
- premium seal;
- pattern anchor;
- loading / boot emblem;
- environmental mark.

## 24.2 Secondary monogram — SPARK

Form:
- four-point star / flare;
- elongated vertical axis;
- inward-curved sides;
- precise bilateral/radial feeling;
- visually derived from the same curve language as the primary mark.

Meaning:
**insight, opportunity, luck, ignition, singular breakthrough.**

Use as:
- secondary emblem;
- punctuation;
- pattern companion;
- premium-state marker;
- milestone marker;
- transition device.

**Important:** generic sparkle icons remain prohibited. This specific proprietary Spark monogram is the only approved sparkle-like brand form.

## 24.3 Geometry cleanup contract

Before treating any redrawn mark as canonical production geometry:

1. rebuild as vectors, not traced raster paths;
2. establish one geometric construction grid;
3. normalize curve tension across both marks;
4. optical-correct rather than forcing mathematically perfect but visually weak symmetry;
5. preserve silhouette at 16–24 px;
6. avoid hairline negative spaces that collapse at app-icon scale;
7. preserve the family resemblance between Structure and Spark;
8. maintain clean one-color masters before material treatment;
9. test black-on-white, white-on-black, emboss, foil, 3D, and 16 px;
10. never modify the geometry merely to make a single render “look more 3D.”

The **vector shape is identity truth**.  
Any 3D/material render is a contextual expression of that truth and must not redefine the geometry.

## 24.4 Clear space

Let `M` equal the short-axis width of the standalone mark.

Recommended:
- standard clear space: `0.25M`;
- prestige/hero use: `0.5M+`;
- never allow body copy or another logo inside the clear space.

Intentional editorial cropping is allowed only when:
- at least 60% of the mark remains;
- the silhouette is still recognizable;
- the crop is obviously compositional, not accidental.

## 24.5 Minimum sizes

Flat digital:
- Spark: target ≥ 16 px if the negative space survives;
- Structure: target ≥ 20 px;
- below this, use optically simplified micro masters if required.

3D rendered:
- do not use a detailed hero render below a size where its material response becomes visual noise;
- switch to flat/duotone micro master instead.

## 24.6 Canonical finishes

### Flat master
- `paper-50` on dark;
- `ink-1000` on light;
- canonical brand pink when explicitly called for by the identity composition;
- single room accent only for a contextual sub-brand use.

### High-relief 3D
Use when the monogram is rendered as an object:
- hyperrealistic depth;
- contextually appropriate material;
- strong silhouette;
- controlled studio reflections;
- no forced rarity texture.

### Emboss / deboss
Low-contrast graphite, paper, stone, metal, or another contextually appropriate surface.

### Etched
Thin contour derived from master geometry.

## 24.7 Monogram pattern grammar

The two marks form a proprietary repeat system analogous in **recall function** to heritage luxury-house monograms, but the pattern itself must be original.

Approved pattern units:
1. Structure only.
2. Spark only.
3. Alternating Structure / Spark.
4. Structure with Spark as interstitial.
5. Overscaled crop pattern for editorial use.

Default pattern pitch:
- utility surface: `1.75M–2.5M`;
- brand/editorial: `1.25M–2.25M`;
- never pack marks until their negative spaces merge visually.

Approved transformations:
- 0°;
- 180° only when the construction remains intentional;
- mirrored use only if a specific tile has been formally approved.

Do not:
- randomly rotate each mark;
- scatter marks like confetti;
- stretch or skew the mark;
- outline some instances and fill others without a system;
- create a faux-fashion pattern by merely repeating the mark densely.

## 24.8 Pattern intensity by context

### Product utility
- 2–6% perceived contrast;
- emboss, etched, matte/gloss differential;
- background only.

### Premium gameplay
- 5–12% perceived contrast;
- selective high-quality material/light response allowed;
- never behind critical small text.

### Brand/editorial
- 8–20% perceived contrast;
- larger scale;
- can interact with photography/3D space;
- may use premium contextual material/rendering selectively.

### Milestone / packaging-like moments
- can become dominant for a short scene;
- still preserve hierarchy.

## 24.9 App icon

Preferred app-icon strategy:
- Structure monogram;
- no wordmark;
- deep graphite or pearl background;
- high-relief contextually rendered Structure mark;
- generous safe area;
- no tiny text;
- no redundant sparkles.

The Spark may be used as alternate/special-state iconography, not as a generic replacement everywhere.

## 24.10 Wordmark relationship

Wordmark:
`ONE PERSON UNICORN`

The wordmark should be typographic and disciplined.

Lockup behavior:
- Structure may precede or sit above wordmark;
- Spark can act as a secondary punctuation device only when needed;
- do not force both marks into every lockup;
- the pattern system carries recall across surfaces where the full lockup is unnecessary.

## 24.11 Brand recall test

A viewer should be able to identify the brand from any **two** of:
- Structure monogram;
- Spark monogram;
- proprietary pattern;
- contextual hyperrealistic 3D object language;
- canonical typography;
- MACHINE/SKY contrast.

If recognition depends on writing the product name in every frame, the system is not working.

# 29B. Final creative-director test

Before shipping any asset or screen, ask:

1. **Could this belong to another AI startup by changing the logo?**  
   If yes, it is too generic.

2. **Could this belong to a crypto app?**  
   If yes, reduce neon, glass, gradients and speculative-tech clichés.

3. **Is the monogram, 3D object, or rarity material doing meaningful work, or merely decorating?**  
   If decoration only, remove or rethink.

4. **Can a player understand what to do without reading a paragraph?**  
   If no, fix interaction.

5. **Can a player understand why the number changed?**  
   If no, fix causality.

6. **Is the most beautiful element also the most important element?**  
   If not, hierarchy is wrong.

7. **Does mobile feel intentionally composed?**  
   If no, redesign rather than shrink.

8. **Is there one memorable emotional beat, or twenty competing effects?**  
   Keep one.

9. **Does the 3D object feel art-directed by the same brand without being forced into the same material?**  
   If no, fix semantic choice, camera, silhouette, render quality or lighting.

10. **Would the work remain recognizable with the wordmark hidden?**  
    If no, strengthen the owned system.
